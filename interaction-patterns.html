Documented interaction decisions — not new UI. Each pattern pairs a live playground with the reasoning behind it, so “which approach do we use here?” has an answer you can see and feel. Change the situation on the left; the guidance updates and you can trigger the real interaction on the right.

Destructive actions

When someone deletes something, how much friction is right? Two defensible answers: a confirm modal that blocks until they say “yes”, or an optimistic delete with an Undo toast that trusts the action and offers a way back. The right call depends on how reversible and how wide-reaching the delete is.

    Confirm modal

    Recommended here

    Blocks the flow with an “Are you sure?” dialog. Deliberate, interruptive, unmissable.

    Use when

    • The action is permanent or genuinely hard to reverse.
    • It affects many items at once (bulk).
    • Consequences are severe or hard to undo (billing, access, data loss).

    Costs

    • Interrupts the flow on every use — nags if the action is common.
    • Confirmation fatigue: people click “Delete” without reading.

    Undo toast

    Recommended here

    Removes the item immediately and offers a time-boxed “Undo”. Trusts the action, respects flow.

    Use when

    • The action is reversible (soft-delete, restore from Trash).
    • It’s a single item and low-stakes.
    • It happens often — friction would compound.

    Costs

    • The window is easy to miss — needs a real, reliable restore.
    • Not safe for irreversible or wide-reaching actions.

    Rule of thumb. Default to an Undo toast for reversible, single-item actions — it respects flow. Escalate to a confirm modal when the action is permanent, affects many items, or is genuinely hard to reverse. Never use both for the same action.
